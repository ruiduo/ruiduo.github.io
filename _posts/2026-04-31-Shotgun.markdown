You can usually tell within a lot of minutes whether a person would be a good lifetime partner, but in just five, I can tell you whether I'd enjoy someone riding shotgun in my car or if you should jimmy open the glove compartment and use the sawed-off inside to match my car's interior with the paint job. It was summer of my sophomore year, and I had just hauled my sexy maroon Honda Accord across 20 hours of West Texas desert so I could cruise around in Phoenix like a cool cat. After meeting my cohort, I took two of them to In-N-Out for dinner. There was my roommate, Ben, and there was Adam. Adam was a Code Buckshot kind of guy, as in, I had just spent an excruciating thirty minutes with Adam trying to pretend my animal fries were much more interesting than just slop on potatoes. Ben had defaulted to the passenger seat on the drive in, but as we were walking back to the parking lot, I heard, "Shotgun!" Fuck. Adam had just called shotgun, and I had an entire bandolier of problems with this. For one, I had actually left my Second Amendment at home so gently recusing myself from the situation and all over the parking lot was out of the question, and for two, Adam sits up so straight he looks like a doped-up meerkat. I knew this would bother me for the entire ride back. Every time I'd pretend to be a diligent driver and check my rearview mirror, I would notice him sitting erect, swiveling his skull around like the local ATC tower, and I don't want any of that federal posture in my passenger seat.

I was used to bad passengers though. My mom, for example, is also a terrible passenger seat companion. We were driving on I-5 south from Sequoia National Park to Los Angeles before coming to a dead stop in bumper-to-bumper traffic. "What's that?" calmly inquired my mom is what I wish had happened. Instead, it was more like "Holy shit Richard OMG what is that alien thing? AHHHH!" which, obviously, would freak me the fuck out. I mean who knows what type of religious missionary would be knocking on windows in the Utah adjacent sands we're in. Before kicking it into high gear, I looked outside my window to see that the terrifying spawn of Area-51 in question was a jubilant ball of tumbleweed. "Don't let it hit the car, don't let it hit the car, Richard!" shouted my mom. "Mother, the lightweight organic matter would do minimal damage to our solid metal death machine, and furthermore, we're completely deadlocked in heavy traffic. Thus, we have relinquished control of its collision trajectory with the car to fate," is what I could've said, but I just grumbled in response and tried to appear ultra focused on the unchanging road conditions. 

Another pet peeve of mine in passengers seat riders is when they fall asleep. Now, if it was two of us driving long haul across the states, completely a-okay to snooze a little, but if you staked claim to shotgun in a group setting, you better cut up a line or two of coke with my expired license before you doze off, especially if I, too, am ready to let a nondenominational divinity take the wheel. Now, my friends and I were driving back in the midnight hours from The Rattlesnake Saloon which was a good three hours from our AirBNB. We weren't out late at The Rattlesnake Saloon for the riveting entertainment or the crowd-pleasing live music; instead, we were stuck waiting for our food because the head chef decided to have a heart attack halfway through his shift. Our burgers afterward weren't even worth the wait. I mean can we please defibrillate the chef de cuisine and get him back on the line – clearly, he's the only one who knew his shit. Before long into the drive back, my friends in the rear seats had fallen asleep, so to entertain ourselves, my passenger, John, and I started talking about the scariest thing that could appear on the road. We joked about werewolves or an abandoned escape pod, before John goes, "the scariest thing to see would probably be exact replicas of ourselves." Good one John. That would be pretty scary, and now, I have to get my faux leather seats disinfected. 

Passengers have a whole lot more responsibilities than just looking pretty. The Mississippian car's natural predator is the white-tailed deer. Whereas the deer's two natural predators are tiny metal zinging at 100mph and a whole lot more metal speeding at 100mph blasting Soulja Boy. As I grew up, it was courtesy for the passenger to play the Deer Game. The Deer Game is relatively simple, if it's nighttime and you see deer on the side of the road, you calmly and politely let the driver know so they can slow down and not paint their very new car with venison and fur. Now, I was driving a couple of hours back home after picking up a friend from out of town, and a bit after sunset, we turned onto this back road complete with shoddy lighting and a whole lot of forest. Before long, I called out "1-0" as I had just spotted my first deer. My out-of-town friend expressed confusion at the keeping of score. Oh, I thought, maybe not everyone played this game as a kid, so I kindly explained to him the rules, "just let me know if you see a deer and update the score." The score got up to "5-0" before I wondered if something was utterly wrong with my passenger. Did he have Lasik surgery recently? He really wasn't blinking a whole lot. Did he not know what deer were? Or, maybe, has he never played a game before? Being from out of town, I thought not recognizing deer would be the more believable reason, so I reminded him that deer were like smaller fuzzier horses. This factoid, however, did not help his performance. I won't be riding with him again, unless it's hunting season.

"So does this mean I get shotgun?" Adam asks again to snap me back into the parking lot. The problem was it totally did mean he got shotgun. For my entire driving career, I awarded the passenger seat to whoever called it out first which is how the ancient Roman charioteers decreed it to be when they awarded the passenger floorboard to whoever called "Javelin!" first. However, I had just met Adam, and he didn't know that I was a fair and meritocratic ruler. So, I began to scheme. "Sadly Adam," I started strongly, "shotgun in my car goes to the shortest member of the passengers," I finished poorly. "Something about moving up in the world," I coughed out to cover up my faulty logic.
