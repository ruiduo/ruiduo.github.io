Glory to the Saybricked, for I did that shit for us. Every week for an entire year, I plundered the booty of the condom chest. There must be some head CCE tracking the condoms consumed per college statistic, and I thought that if I amassed enough of those squelchy single use fuckers that the legend would even reach old Pericles's ear. I needed everyone to know that Saybrook College gets the most play. I could see it in my minds eye. A McInnis email addressed to all Yalies with a bar graph that ranked the rescos by condoms consumed, and on the far right would be Saybrook's tower overshadowing all the rest, twice as thick as Harkness. Don't you see it now? For our pride, you could find me shoving 50 something condoms in my tight jean pockets and squelching back up the stairs. 

But what to do with all the condoms? I couldn't just have them collect dust, that wouldn't be the Saybrook way. Of course, there was the obvious answer, but I wasn't planning to throw any of those types of parties, besides, balloons are so 2000. So, I began to experiment. You've seen the world's largest rubber band ball, but I rebuilt the Eiffel Tower one layer of French Durex at a time. I started a YouTube channel dedicated to performing flavored condom mukbangs. My floor was so littered with opened condom wrappers that my roommate thought I was searching for the Golden Ticket for my Willy Wonka. By the time I exhausted half of my collection, I even managed to save Christmas twice: Once by providing stockings for stuffers, and the second time, I substituted Santa's broken sack with a Magnum. 

Condoms had throttled every aspect of my life. A little fun fact about me: I sort my condoms, when I get them, into colors like M&M's. So in this period of my life, I often color coordinated my outfit down to the matching rubber on my pecker, and instead of a Durex in my wallet, when I went out in public I paid with a condom filled with jangling coins.

But still with half my wrapper wealth left, I ran out of ideas on how to use them, so I began to browse the internet. It felt like every other collectible hobby had a community, yet I couldn't even find a single Ebay listing of a (Like New) condom. However, I did run into some luck posting on public protection forums about all of my favorite brands of protection, but I'd always be contacted by some nut asking if I too "rubbed off the serial numbers." "What serial numbers?" I'd politely inquire. "The ones by the safety," they'd clarify. "The whole thing's for safety!" I'd exclaim. "That's what I keep TRYING to tell THEM," they'd concur. Until I finally realized, and I had to say, "Sorry sir, I don't collect guns, just holsters." 

Deflated, I retreated back to my normal life at college, back to emptying the condom container whenever it got refilled. However, it seemed my original goal of spreading the Saybrook name had succeeded! At parties, girls would suddenly come up to me and ask if they could go back to my place. Of course, I knew these were just thinly veiled attempts to check out my awesome condom collection. I didn't think the niche condom connoisseur community would be so popular at Yale, but I happily obliged. When we got back to my room, I showed off my rarities: 2026 Winter Olympics, AAdvantage Executive Platinum®, old timey leather ones. But they always tried to open them like no ma'am. Those are mint first edition. Let's use the unflavored ones.
